How to read this

The framework behind the scores

Demographics decide the economy

Zeihan's core claim: people consume most from 30 to 44 (homes, kids, cars) and save most from 45 to 64. When the second group retires, the capital dries up; when the first group shrinks, the consumers do. A country's age structure is its economic forecast, and it can't be changed on a policy timeline.

  • Prime consumers (30–44) drive domestic demand.
  • Capital-rich (45–64) fund investment and cheap credit.
  • Retirees (65+) draw down savings and need imports of labor.

The Order is ending

Since 1945 the US Navy has guaranteed global shipping so that anyone could trade with anyone. Zeihan argues the US no longer needs that bargain and is stepping back. Export-led economies that need open sea lanes, imported energy and imported food are the exposed ones. Consumption-led, resource-secure, continental economies are the insulated ones.

What the outlook score means

A 1–10 synthesis of Zeihan's stated views across his books and talks: demographic runway, energy and food security, trade exposure, and geography. 9–10 is insulated and growing; 5–6 is holding through managed decline; 1–2 is a system he expects to break within a generation. It is an editorial reading of one analyst's forecast, not a consensus and not a model output.

Population, median age, fertility and age-structure figures are rounded approximations of the UN World Population Prospects 2024 medium variant; GDP, growth, consumption and export shares approximate IMF and World Bank 2024–25 data. Working-age, consumer and capital cohort changes are computed from the age bands shown. Zeihan's forecasts are contested by many demographers and economists; this page presents his framework, not an endorsement of it.
